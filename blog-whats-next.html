What is coming next for Candy Mass

7 October 2026 · 4 min read

This is an early release, and there is a list of things we want to do next. Here it is, roughly in the order we intend to do them, with honest notes on what is likely to slip.

1. Better artwork

The current sprites were drawn at a fairly low resolution, and on a modern high-density phone screen they are softer than they should be. Replacing them with higher-resolution artwork is the single biggest visual improvement available, and it is the thing we are working on first.

This also means the baskets and the particle effects get a pass at the same time. When it lands, the game should look noticeably sharper without changing how it plays at all.

2. More languages

The game is currently English only. The plan is to add Arabic, Hindi and Spanish first — chosen because they open up large audiences with genuinely different reading habits, not because they are easy.

Arabic is the complicated one, because it reads right-to-left and the entire interface has to mirror. It is also the language most likely to be delayed, for exactly that reason.

The design intent is that the game detects your device language and just uses it. There will be no language menu, except in India, where English is kept as the default with an option to switch to Hindi.

3. A daily challenge

Once a day, a fixed challenge that every player gets the same version of, with its own leaderboard. The point is to give the people who want a reason to come back a small, bounded one — a two-minute thing, not a daily chore with a streak that punishes you for missing a day.

4. Star ratings per round

Three stars per round, awarded based on how many lives you finished with. This is a small feature with an outsized effect: it gives players a reason to replay a round they have already cleared, which is the cheapest source of extra playtime a game like this can have.

It also makes progress legible at a glance. A row of stars is a clearer summary of how you have been doing than a round number.

5. Small difficulty and variety additions

A handful of things that make later rounds more interesting without making them more stressful: rounds where bombs fall noticeably faster than candy, occasional double drops, and an early warning before a busy stretch. All small, all reversible if they turn out to be annoying.

What we are deliberately not doing

It is more useful to list what is staying out than what is going in.

  • No energy system. Ever. You can play as much as you want.
  • No forced sign-in. Guest play stays fully supported.
  • No pay-to-win. Nothing purchasable will make the game easier.
  • No banner ads. Rewarded ads stay optional, and interstitial ads stay between rounds.
  • No daily chore mechanics. A missed day will never be punished.

About timing

We are a very small studio and we would rather ship things that work than ship things on a date. If a feature is not ready, it does not go out. The artwork and languages are the most likely to take longer than expected.

If something on this list matters to you more than the rest, email us and say so. It genuinely affects the order.
